# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Sift is for people with food allergies and the caregivers, partners, parents, and family members who help them make dining decisions. They use it while planning a meal, reading an unfamiliar restaurant menu, traveling, or standing at the table with limited time and incomplete ingredient information. Their job is not to obtain a verdict from software; it is to notice possible concerns and ask restaurant staff better questions before ordering.

## Product Purpose

Sift turns a user-selected menu photo and allergy profile into conservative, practical guidance. It surfaces possible listed allergens, common hidden-risk patterns, image or information uncertainty, cross-contact concerns, and questions that can be confirmed with restaurant staff. The website's job is to make that value immediately understandable, prove it with authentic product screens, explain how Sift handles uncertainty, and provide the complete support, safety, privacy, and legal information expected of a launched consumer app.

Success means a visitor can understand what Sift does, what it cannot do, why its AI is designed conservatively, and how to get or contact Sift without encountering a waitlist, fabricated proof, or an implied food-safety guarantee.

## Brand Personality

Calm, protective, and exact. Sift speaks plainly and with care. It should feel composed in a high-stakes moment without becoming clinical, alarmist, cute, or overconfident. Confidence comes from visible evidence, transparent limits, and useful next actions.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI startup templates: gradient meshes, glowing orbs, AI badges, fake dashboards, invented metrics, and vague claims about intelligence.
- The V1 website's cream-and-serif editorial template: repeated tiny uppercase kickers, numbered scaffolding used as decoration, oversized radii, glass navigation, broad soft shadows, and reconstructed phone UI.
- Waitlist marketing: early-access forms, email capture cards, “coming soon” calls to action, and launch theater.
- Fear-based allergy marketing or binary “safe to eat” language.
- Outdated, reconstructed, or invented app screenshots presented as the real product.
- Unsupported accuracy percentages, fabricated testimonials, press logos, or customer counts.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the real product.** Authentic current app captures are the primary proof; decorative mockups never substitute for product evidence.
2. **Make uncertainty useful.** Explain what Sift saw, what it inferred, what remains unknown, and what the diner should ask next.
3. **Practice protective clarity.** Use calm hierarchy, readable language, and visible limitations rather than alarmist color or false certainty.
4. **Earn every claim.** Reliability statements must be tied to a dated method, evaluation evidence, or an explicit known limitation.
5. **Feel native to dining.** Menus, table-side conversation, visible annotations, and real restaurant context should shape the page more than technology-company conventions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA across the website. Maintain readable contrast, visible keyboard focus, semantic landmarks and headings, descriptive alt text, 44px minimum touch targets where practical, and predictable navigation. Respect reduced-motion preferences. Never communicate risk through color alone; pair every state with text and, where useful, an icon or structural label. Use plain language that remains useful for anxious, time-limited readers and support responsive layouts from small phones through large desktops.
